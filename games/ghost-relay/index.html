Ghost Relay · SpookyQubits · A teleportation teaching game

The relay rules

Deliver one sealed state

  1. Send two radio bits. Alice’s measurement gives her a Z / Sign bit and an X / Flip bit. Bob needs the labels, because the bits can arrive in either order.
  2. Listen to the radio. Wait for both bits if you can. A “?” means a bit is missing. A flagged bit might be wrong. You get two resend stamps for the whole shift, and each stamp gets you a reliable copy of one bit. Waiting for the next frame costs nothing.
  3. Set Bob’s switches. A 1 means turn that switch on. A 0 means leave it off. You can go back to the radio before you finish. Deliver all six states and check the replay to see how you did.

You earn 3 points for getting both switches right, up to 18. A lucky guess can score, and the replay tells you when that happened. A fully checked delivery needs both bits with all flags resolved. The two questions at the end have their own score.

Why does Bob need both bits?

Alice and Bob start with an entangled pair. Alice combines her half of that pair with the input qubit, then measures. Her Bell step uses a controlled-NOT and a Hadamard before the two measurements. Afterward, she has two random bits and the original state is gone from her side. Bob’s particle is already with him. His switches use Alice’s bits to put his qubit in the right state.

Try No bits and then Both bits in the Relay lab. With no message, Bob’s local measurements look random for every starting state. The bits still have to travel to him through an ordinary channel.

The replay can compare the exact starting and ending states because the phone simulated both. In a real experiment, a single measurement on an unknown qubit gives far less information. The erased bit, the 25% and 50% flip chances, and the stamp budget are rules for this game.

Share one phone

Play as Sender and Receiver. With three people, let a Courier manage the radio and choose when to spend stamps. Pass the phone on the covered screens and let the Receiver work from the radio messages. Swap jobs after a shift.
